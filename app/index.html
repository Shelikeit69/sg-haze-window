How the estimate works

  1. Read the latest NEA 1-hour PM2.5 for the region.
  2. Take the CAMS forecast for each coming hour and scale it by how far CAMS has been off from NEA readings since 1 August ().
  3. Give each hour an estimate that is half the current reading and half the scaled forecast. The suggested window is the daytime hour with the lowest forecast.

How well it worked in testing

The machine-learning model

We trained a gradient-boosting model on NEA readings, weather, NASA satellite fire counts and CAMS, using August to November of 2023, 2024 and 2025 plus August to October 2026. It did not beat simply repeating the current reading: 13 to 24 hours ahead it missed by µg/m³ on average, against for repeating the reading. Singapore had almost no haze in 2023 to 2025, so the model has seen only one real haze season.

It now runs in shadow mode. It logs a forecast every day and does not affect the advice. It will be switched on only after it meets criteria we wrote down before any scoring began: at least 30 haze days, a clear win over repeating the reading, and honest uncertainty ranges. Latest shadow run: .

Limits

  • We could not confirm that past CAMS values were published before the hours they forecast, so test results that use CAMS may look better than real use.
  • Estimates are for NEA's five regions. Your street can differ.
  • Smoke can arrive fast. On 14 October the west went from 111 µg/m³ at noon to 284 at 14:00. Check the live reading before you go out.
  • This page does not give medical advice. If you have a heart or lung condition, follow NEA's health advisory and your doctor.
